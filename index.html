What it does

The panels
  • Two panels with tabs, side by side or one above the other, a folder tree, and full or brief listings.
  • Quick search by typing a name, a quick filter (⌘F), history back and forward, and favourite folders (⌘D).
  • Tabs locked to a folder: wander off freely, and coming back to the tab brings the folder back with it.
  • Colour rules by mask, age and size. The branch (⌃B): every file below a folder in one flat list, with every command working on it.
  • Folder sizes on demand with the space bar, or all of them at once.
  • The disks on ⌥F1 and ⌥F2, with their free space; ⌘E ejects one, and the header of every panel shows how much room is left.
Selecting
  • Mark with the space bar — marked files turn red, as in Total Commander.
  • Select by mask, invert, select the same extension, put a selection aside and bring it back, or select the names listed in a file.
Files
  • Copy, move and delete (F5, F6, F8) with progress, conflicts and cancelling, and a background queue you can pause and reorder.
  • Delete to the Trash, delete for good, or overwrite and delete — with a plain word about what that is worth on an SSD.
  • Multi-rename with patterns, counters, find and replace with regular expressions and changes of case, all previewed before anything happens — and the last rename can be taken back.
  • Attributes and timestamps, split and join, and SHA-256 and BLAKE3 checksums, created and verified.
  • Drag files out to Finder, Mail or a browser, and drop them in from anywhere: a drop opens the copy or move dialog with the folder already filled in.
  • Symbolic and hard links (⌃⇧F5), made in the other panel’s folder.
Comparing
  • Compare two folders and synchronize them: a direction for every pair, extras deleted only if you ask, and a check afterwards.
  • Compare two files line by line, with the changed part of each line highlighted, in any encoding — and an honest answer for binaries and very large files.
Archives
  • An archive opens like a folder. ZIP and TAR are read and written; TAR.GZ, TAR.XZ, RAR and 7z are read.
  • Encrypted ZIP read and packed with AES-256 (⌥F5). The password never goes on a command line; it stays for the session, or in the Keychain if you ask.
Finding things
  • Search (⌥F7) by name, mask, size, date and content — as text or as a regular expression — inside archives too.
  • The results go into the panel, where every command works on them. Searches can be saved.
  • A duplicate finder that decides by content and never selects a whole group for you.
Servers
  • SFTP with a password or a key: a server behaves like a folder, with the same keys as the disk. Your servers are one ⌘K away.
Viewing and the rest
  • A built-in viewer (F3) for text, hex and images, with encodings and search, and Quick Look on ⌥Space.
  • A command line under the panels, a real terminal below them, and a terminal opened in the folder you are in.
  • A button bar, your own commands, and every keyboard shortcut yours to change.
  • Quick view (⌃Q): the other panel shows the file under the cursor — images, PDF, video, documents — and follows it.
Not yet
No plugins (Total Commander’s are Windows libraries), no FTP or WebDAV, no thumbnails or custom columns, no printing. Folders with more than about 20,000 files list noticeably slower.

Questions

The F-keys change the brightness instead

Most Mac keyboards give F1–F12 to brightness and volume. Hold fn with them, or turn on “Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys” in System Settings → Keyboard. Every command is in the menus as well, and every shortcut can be changed.

Why does it mention Full Disk Access?

macOS asks about your Desktop, Documents and external disks by itself, the first time the app looks there. ~/Library, other apps’ data and Time Machine need Full Disk Access, which only you can grant, in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Eagle Commander shows you where at first launch, and works without it everywhere else.

How do updates work?

Eagle Commander asks once whether it may look for new versions, and never installs one without you. A licence covers every 1.x update.

Is it Total Commander for the Mac?

No. It is a program of its own, made for the Mac, that borrows Total Commander’s keys and habits. It is not affiliated with Total Commander or its author.
